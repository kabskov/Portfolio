If I could start over, I would probably change a few things... But let's start with what actually worked.
 
### What worked
- **Planning early.** I planned what to do each week and put it on a Kanban board in a GitHub project. In the beginning it gave me a good overview.
- **Writing down my limitations.** I wrote the scope limitations very early. When I got absorbed in the project and wanted to add everything, having them in black and white helped me stay focused.

### What didn't work
- **I started late.** I only began on the stone collector idea about three weeks into the semester. If I had chosen it from the start, the project could have followed the lessons much better.
- **I lost the overview.** Over time I got more and more stressed, partly because of things outside school, and it became harder to keep track of everything. I stopped updating the Kanban board because it took time I did not have, and I had to take more and longer breaks than I had planned.
- **I did not have a complete plan.** I would have liked a finished class diagram from the beginning, with all the classes and methods I needed to implement. It is hard for me to build that much overview on my own while I am still learning, and it got even harder under stress.

### What I would have liked to include
More validation (password length and email format) and image upload. Both are in the README as known limitations.
 
### What I'd do differently 
- Choose the project on day one.
- Make a full class diagram before writing code (if possible).
- Keep the Kanban board much simpler, so it is easy to keep updating, even in a busy week.
- Plan for breaks, so they do not throw the whole schedule off.
