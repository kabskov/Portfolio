You've just spent the last couple of months knitting or crocheting your dream sweater. Now you're finally done! You're proud and happy to see the result of your hard work. But what's that? That fluff at the bottom of your project bag? You reach for it, grab it and bring it out into the light. It's half a skein of yarn! "Not again," you mutter to yourself. You take the scrap yarn and do the walk of shame over to your yarn drawer. You place the yarn scraps in the drawer and struggle to get it shut again: it's already stuffed with leftover yarn you have no idea what to do with. "I'll figure something out eventually," you think and walk away from the drawer.

## The Problem

This scenario probably sounds familiar to a lot of knitters and crocheters. The scrap yarn piles up, and you don't know what to do with it. You don't want to just throw it out either, since the yarn cost money and resources to produce. So what do you do?

The obvious answer is, of course, to find a pattern that can use up the leftover yarn - but that's where a new problem shows up.

## Where Do You Find Patterns?

There are plenty of ways to find knitting and crochet patterns. You could, for example:

- Go to the library (if you're an old soul)
- Check Instagram
- Search Pinterest
- Search good old Google
- Search websites or apps built for knitters and/or crocheters

What all of these have in common, though, is that you're not searching exclusively among patterns for smaller projects and thus end up spending a lot of time filtering and sorting through results. Time you'd obviously rather spend knitting or crocheting 🙂. That's exactly the problem ScrapYarn is meant to solve.

## How Will We Solve the Problem?

We want to create a platform full of knitting and crochet patterns for smaller projects, where users will be able to filter by a range of criteria - such as yarn amount, needle/hook size, knitting or crocheting, and skill level - and then see suggested projects that match. The goal is to make it easier for knitters and crocheters to find patterns for projects that don't require much yarn - projects that can be made with scrap yarn, so the leftovers don't go to waste or keep piling up.


> An app that makes it quick and easy to find knitting and crochet patterns for smaller projects - so you can finally use up all the scrap yarn piling up at home.

## What's the setup for the project?

Our third semester project mirrors how software is built in practice: incrementally, with new requirements, new technologies, and continuous reflection.

**The Core Idea**

We will:
- Choose our own application idea
- Build it incrementally over the semester
- Extend the application with new technologies and reflect on our progress each week

**Timeline for the backend**

Weeks 1-10: The goal after week 10 is a production-ready backend API. During these weeks, we'll gradually add:

- Java + Maven project structure
- REST API and endpoints
- JPA / database persistence
- External API integration
- Validation and error handling
- Integration tests
- JWT-based authentication and authorization
- Configuration and environment variables
- Deployment

This project is designed to help us answer a very real question: 
> Can I actually build and evolve a backend system?
